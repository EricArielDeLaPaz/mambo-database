# Color-blindness prevalence research — figures for "people overall" (accessed 2026-11-14)

Goal: replace "1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women" wording in the guides with figures about **people overall**, per type: red-green CVD overall; deuteranomaly and deuteranopia; protanomaly and protanopia; tritan types; achromatopsia.

Method: only official or primary sources (National Eye Institute, MedlinePlus Genetics / NCBI Bookshelf, Colour Blind Awareness, an open-access peer-reviewed review that reproduces the Webvision prevalence table). Every number below is quoted from the page it links to, or computed with the arithmetic shown. Nothing is from a blog. All pages accessed 2026-11-14.

Per-type sex-split rates for red-green CVD (Webvision table, via the Aveh review, Table 3; "All numbers in the table are percentages"): Protanomaly 1% males / 0.01% females; Deuteranomaly 5% / 0.4%; Protanopia 1% / 0.01%; Deuteranopia 1.5% / 0.01%. Colour Blind Awareness gives the male split only: "The 8% of colour blind men with inherited colour blindness can be divided approximately into 1% deuteranopes, 1% protanopes, 1% protanomalous and 5% deuteranomalous."

## Computed overall figures (assumption: an even male/female split)

The rule from the plan: compute only from sex-split inputs, show the arithmetic, label it "computed". Formula: (male rate + female rate) / 2.

## Why it differs by sex (X chromosome), neutral wording, cited

- NEI, "Causes of Color Vision Deficiency" — https://nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness/causes-color-vision-deficiency: "Parents pass down the genes for red-green color vision deficiency through the X chromosome." "Males have only 1 X chromosome from their mother. If that X chromosome has the gene for red-green color vision deficiency, the child will have red-green color vision deficiency." "Females have 2 X chromosomes... To inherit red-green color vision deficiency, both X chromosomes must carry the gene... It's rare for this to happen." And: "Blue-yellow color vision deficiency and complete color vision deficiency involve other chromosomes, so they affect people of different sexes equally."

## Notes and caveats

- The 8%/0.5% and per-type numbers describe populations of predominantly Northern European ancestry most strongly; Colour Blind Awareness: figures "rise in areas where there are a greater number of white (Caucasian) people at the head of population, so in Scandinavia the figures increase to approximately 10-11% of men. By contrast, in sub-Saharan Africa there are few colour blind people." The Aveh review: "A larger percentage of colour deficiencies have been found in those of European origin (8% in men and 0.5% in women) as compared to male of African (3% – 4%) or Asian (3%) origin."
- The "computed" overall figures assume an even male/female split of the population, which is close to true worldwide but not exact in every country.
- A 2025 narrative review on PMC (PMC12385717) was checked and rejected: its summary table mislabels protan/deutan inheritance as autosomal and swaps subtype numbers; do not use it.
- The site's color blindness guide currently says "1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women" (National Eye Institute wording); the guides' "men" to "people" edits should use only the figures above.
